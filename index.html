Bio

About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Florida State University, advised by Prof. Xin Yuan. My research focuses on communication optimization in high-performance computing systems, with an emphasis on large-scale interconnection networks.

I develop and evaluate techniques for adaptive routing, process mapping, congestion mitigation, and resource placement, using packet- and MPI-level simulation across traditional HPC and AI workloads. My broader goal is to help design efficient and scalable next-generation HPC systems. Before my current HPC work, I developed AI-enabled educational simulations and worked as a software engineer.

Research

Research directions

01

Process Mapping

Topology-aware mapping for Dragonfly systems that balances communication locality with network-load distribution and physical placement.

02

Adaptive Routing

Routing mechanisms that respond to congestion and changing traffic conditions, including software-defined and learning-based approaches.

03

Allocation & Interference

Ongoing work on node allocation, fragmented system allocations, and mitigating performance interference among concurrently running jobs.

04

Simulation & Evaluation

Packet- and MPI-level performance studies with SST and BookSim across HPC motifs, AI communication patterns, dynamic traffic, and topology variants.

05

Systems for AI

Communication-efficient execution of AI workloads on large-scale systems, including collective communication and network-level behavior.

06

AI for Systems

Learning-based methods for systems optimization, including adaptive decision-making for routing and network configuration.
