02 · Research

What I work on — and why.

My research starts from a simple systems question: when thousands of communicating processes share a large machine, how can the network, runtime, and placement decisions work together instead of becoming bottlenecks?

01

Process Mapping for Dragonfly Networks

I study how an application's communication graph should be mapped onto the node–router–group hierarchy of Dragonfly systems. Locality alone is not enough: a mapping can reduce communication distance while still concentrating traffic, inducing local gateway traffic, or placing logical partitions poorly on the physical topology.

  • Evaluated heuristic, metaheuristic, and graph-partitioning approaches under a common Dragonfly setting.
  • Developed DraCO-Map, a hierarchical partition–refine–place framework that considers locality, network load, induced traffic, and physical placement.
  • Evaluate traditional HPC and AI workloads using SST and production-style ALCF traces on representative large-system configurations.
02

Adaptive & Learning-Based Routing

Routing is where my AI for Systems direction is most explicit. I investigate how routing can adapt to congestion and changing traffic while respecting the hard constraints of the physical network.

  • Investigated software-defined mechanisms for improving UGAL routing, contributing to our CCGrid 2025 work.
  • Studied why constrained MARL routing is difficult: path enumeration quality, action feasibility, and learning dynamics all affect what the agent can actually learn.
  • Developed a constraint-aware MARL framework and the Throughput Degradation Factor (TDF) for characterizing candidate-path quality.
  • Instantiated the framework as UGAL-Q; in the current evaluation it achieves up to 66.47% lower communication time than prior MARL-based Dragonfly routing schemes while approaching the globally informed UGAL-G baseline.
03

Fragmentation-Aware Node Allocation & Inter-Job Interference

Process mapping decides where processes go within an allocation; node allocation asks which machine resources the job should receive in the first place. I am extending my work upstream to this resource-allocation problem.

  • Investigating communication- and fragmentation-aware allocation using group/router resource availability and workload characteristics.
  • Studying when compact allocation is preferable to spreading a job across the network — and when communication structure changes that decision.
  • Extending the analysis to inter-job interference using production-style workload traces, with the goal of making allocation decisions aware of both a job's own communication and the traffic created by its neighbors.
04

Large-Scale Interconnect Simulation & Performance Analysis

I build the experimental infrastructure needed to ask these questions at scale. My work uses both packet-level and MPI-level simulation to connect network mechanisms to end-to-end application behavior.

  • Built and extended workflows using SST Merlin, Ember, Firefly and BookSim.
  • Model traditional HPC motifs and AI/deep-learning communication patterns, including collective-heavy workloads.
  • Study routing, mapping, topology variants, dynamic traffic, congestion, performance variability, and scalability under controlled conditions.
05

Systems for AI

Modern AI increasingly behaves like a distributed systems and networking problem. Training and inference at scale depend on moving large volumes of data efficiently across accelerators and nodes.

  • Use AI workloads alongside traditional HPC workloads when evaluating routing and mapping mechanisms.
  • Study how communication topology, congestion, placement, and network utilization affect distributed AI execution.
  • Long-term goal: co-design communication-aware system mechanisms that let future AI platforms scale efficiently rather than treating the network as an afterthought.
06

AI for Systems

I am equally interested in the reverse direction: using learning as a systems mechanism, but only where it adds value over robust analytical or heuristic methods.

  • Current work includes MARL-based routing and prior exploration of ML-based estimation of UGAL-L routing bias.
  • I am interested in learned policies that adapt to workload, congestion, and system state while remaining constrained by topology and correctness.
  • My broader goal is practical intelligence for systems: learning that is measurable, explainable in system terms, and evaluated against strong non-AI baselines.

Featured research · Process mapping

DraCO-Map

A Dragonfly-specific hierarchical process-mapping framework designed to go beyond locality-only mapping. DraCO-Map decomposes mapping across the network hierarchy and explicitly addresses the gap between logical graph partitioning and physical network placement.

  • Hierarchical partition–refine–place design across Dragonfly groups and routers.
  • Balances communication locality with hotspot/load considerations rather than optimizing edge cut alone.
  • Accounts for induced intra-group traffic and gateway structure when translating logical partitions to physical resources.
  • Evaluated with traditional HPC and AI workloads using large-scale simulation and ALCF production-style traces.

UGAL-Q & Constraint-Aware MARL Routing

Learning-based adaptive routing for Dragonfly networks under real topology constraints.

Implemented an MNIST neural network in C/C++ and progressively accelerated it across multiple levels of parallelism.
